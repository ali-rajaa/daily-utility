## What happens when you delete a photo

Deleting a photo feels final. You tap the bin icon, the picture disappears, and it's gone. On most modern Android phones, though, that's not quite what happens, at least not straight away.

Many gallery and file apps don't erase a photo the moment you delete it. Instead, they move it to a **trash**, **bin** or **recently deleted** folder, where it waits for a set period before being removed for good. It works much like the recycle bin on a computer: a safety net for exactly the moment you realise you've deleted something you wanted to keep.

That safety net is what makes recovery possible. Photo Recover & Data Recovery is built around it: it helps you find deleted photos and files that are still in your phone's trash bin, and restore them to your phone where you can use them again.

This guide explains where deleted photos go, where to look first, and what you can do to give yourself the best chance of getting them back.

## How long deleted photos stay in the trash

How long a deleted photo waits in the trash depends on the app it was deleted from. There's no single rule for every phone, but common examples look like this:

| Where the photo was deleted | Typical time in the trash |
|---|---|
| Google Photos | 60 days for items that were backed up, 30 days for items that weren't |
| Samsung Gallery | 30 days in the Recycle bin |
| Files by Google | 30 days in Trash |
| Other gallery and file apps | Varies; many use 30 days, some have no trash at all |
{: .guide-table}

After that time, items in the trash are deleted automatically. Some apps also empty the trash sooner if your phone is running low on storage, and you can empty it yourself at any time, which removes everything in it straight away.

The key point is that the clock starts when you delete the photo. The sooner you look, the more likely it is to still be there. For an app-by-app list of where deleted photos go, see our guide, [Where Do Deleted Photos Go on Android?](/daily-info/where-deleted-photos-go-android)

## Where to look first

Before anything else, check the places deleted photos most often end up. It's quicker than you might think, and often all it takes.

### Your gallery app's trash

Open the gallery app you use for photos and look for a **Trash**, **Bin**, **Recycle bin** or **Recently deleted** option. It's usually in the app's menu or under a library or albums section. If your photo is there, you can restore it from inside the gallery app.

### Your file manager's trash

If you deleted a file using a file manager app rather than a gallery, check that app's trash too. Some phones have more than one place deleted files can go, so it's worth checking each app you use.

### Your cloud backup

If your photos are backed up to cloud storage, the photo may still be in your backup even after it's gone from your phone. Check the backup's own trash as well: some cloud services keep deleted items there for a while too.

### Chat apps

If you sent the photo to someone, or someone sent it to you, it may still be in a chat. Search the conversation, or ask the person you sent it to. Many chat apps also save received photos to their own folder on your phone.

### Memory card

If your phone has a memory card, check whether the photo was saved there rather than on the phone's internal storage.

## A step-by-step checklist when a photo is missing

When you realise a photo has gone, it's easy to panic and start trying everything at once. Working through the places in order is faster and less stressful.

1. **Stop and don't delete anything else.** Avoid clearing space, emptying trash folders or running cleaning apps.
2. **Search your gallery.** Photos sometimes aren't deleted at all, just moved to a different album or hidden. Search by date or place if your gallery supports it.
3. **Check the gallery's trash or recently deleted folder.** This is where most deleted photos are found.
4. **Check your file manager's trash**, if you delete files that way.
5. **Check your cloud backup**, including its own trash.
6. **Check your chats and email**, for photos you sent or received.
7. **Ask the people who were there.** A friend or family member may have the same photo, or one taken at the same moment.
8. **Use a recovery app** to look through deleted items still in your phone's trash bin.

Most missing photos are found in the first three steps.

## Photos deleted from a memory card

Memory cards work a little differently from a phone's internal storage. If a photo was stored on a card and deleted, whether it went to a trash folder depends on the app you used to delete it. Check that app's trash first.

If your phone has a memory card, be careful when removing it or formatting it. Formatting a card erases everything on it, and a card that's removed while the phone is writing to it can become damaged. If a card suddenly shows as empty or unreadable, stop using it straight away and don't format it, even if the phone suggests it: formatting makes recovery far harder.

## Why acting quickly matters

When it comes to deleted photos, time is the thing that matters most. There are two reasons.

First, as described above, items in the trash are removed automatically after a set period. A photo you deleted yesterday is very likely still there. One you deleted two months ago may not be.

Second, once a photo has left the trash and been permanently deleted, the space it used on your phone's storage is marked as free, and the phone can reuse it for new data. Every new photo, video, app update and download makes it more likely that space has been used for something else.

So if you've just realised a photo is missing:

- **Check the trash straight away**, before anything else.
- **Avoid taking lots of new photos or videos** until you've looked.
- **Hold off on large downloads and app updates** for the moment.
- **Don't empty any trash or run cleaning apps** until you've found what you're looking for.

## What recovery can and can't do

It's important to be realistic about recovery. No app can promise to bring back every photo you've ever deleted, and you should be wary of any that claims it can.

Recovery works best when a deleted photo is still in your phone's trash or recently deleted folder. That's exactly what Photo Recover & Data Recovery is built to help with: finding the deleted photos and files that are still there, and restoring them.

Once a photo has been permanently removed from the trash, getting it back becomes much harder. Modern phones encrypt their storage and are designed to clean up deleted space, which protects your privacy but also means permanently deleted photos are often gone for good. A factory reset erases everything on the phone in the same way.

That's why the best protection against losing photos isn't recovery at all. It's having a backup before anything goes wrong.

## Common myths about deleted photos

A lot of advice about deleted photos is out of date, or was never quite right. A few common myths are worth clearing up.

**"Deleted means gone immediately."** On most modern Android phones, a deleted photo first goes to a trash or recently deleted folder, where it stays for a limited time. That's why it's always worth checking.

**"Anything can be recovered with the right software."** Not on a modern phone. Once a photo has been permanently deleted and the space reused, or the phone has been reset, it's usually gone for good. Be wary of any app or service that promises otherwise.

**"Restarting the phone will bring it back."** Restarting doesn't restore deleted files. It won't hurt, but it won't help either.

**"If it's backed up, deleting it from the phone deletes the backup too."** That depends on the service. A true backup keeps its copy separately, but some services sync deletions, so a photo deleted on the phone is also removed online. Check how your backup handles deletions before you rely on it.

**"The trash keeps things forever."** It doesn't. Items in the trash are removed automatically after a set time, and sometimes sooner when storage runs low.

## Photos that were never on your phone

Sometimes a photo seems to have vanished, but it was never actually saved on the phone at all. This is more common than you might think, and knowing about it can save a lot of searching.

- **Photos kept only in the cloud.** Some photo apps save space by keeping full photos online and only small previews on the phone. If you've signed out of that account, the photos may seem to have disappeared, when they're actually still in the account.
- **Photos seen in a chat but not saved.** Some chat apps don't save received photos to your gallery automatically. The photo is still in the conversation, not in your camera roll.
- **Photos on a different account.** If you have more than one account on your phone, photos may be backed up to a different one from the one you're checking.
- **Hidden or archived photos.** Many gallery apps let you hide or archive photos, which removes them from the main view without deleting them.

Before assuming a photo was deleted, check each of these. It's often simply in a different place.

## Knowing when to stop searching

Sometimes a photo can't be found, however carefully you look. If you've checked every trash folder, your backups, your chats and the people you were with, it may be time to accept that it's gone.

That's hard, especially with photos of people and moments that matter. The most useful thing you can do at that point is make sure it doesn't happen again, by setting up a backup so that every photo you take from now on has a copy somewhere safe.

## Restoring photos you've found

When you find a deleted photo you want back, restore it rather than just viewing it in the trash. Restoring moves it back to your phone's normal storage, where it appears in your gallery again and is no longer counted down for deletion.

After restoring, check the photo opens properly, and consider backing it up straight away so you're not relying on a single copy. If you've restored a lot of photos at once, give your gallery app a few moments to catch up: it may take a little while for everything to appear in the right albums.

## Deleted files, not just photos

Photos get the most attention, but the same approach applies to other files: videos, documents, audio recordings and downloads. Many file managers keep deleted files in a trash folder in the same way galleries do.

Videos deserve a special mention. Because they're large, people often delete them to free up space, and only realise later that one mattered. If you're clearing space, check each video before deleting it, and remember that deleted videos sit in the trash for the same limited time as photos.

## How to avoid losing photos in the future

Recovery is a safety net. A few habits mean you'll rarely need it.

### Back up your photos

A backup keeps a copy of your photos somewhere other than your phone, so a deletion, a broken screen or a lost phone doesn't mean losing them. Our flagship app, [Cloud Storage Backup & Drive](/cloud-storage-backup-drive), backs up your photos, videos, contacts and app data, and lets you restore them when you need them.

### Check before you delete in bulk

Selecting dozens of photos at once to free up space is where mistakes happen. Scroll through your selection before confirming, and never delete a whole album in a hurry.

### Be careful with cleaning apps

Apps that promise to free up space by removing "junk" can sometimes delete more than you expect, and some empty trash folders as part of their clean-up. If you use one, check exactly what it's about to remove.

### Don't rely on the trash as storage

The trash is there to catch mistakes, not to keep things. Anything in it will eventually be deleted automatically. If you might want a photo later, restore it and keep it properly.

### Keep an eye on your storage

A phone that's constantly full is more likely to empty its trash early and push you into deleting things in a hurry. Keeping some free space gives you breathing room, and gives deleted photos their full time in the trash.

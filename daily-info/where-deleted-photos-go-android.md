You tap delete, confirm, and the photo vanishes from your gallery. It feels instant and final. But on most Android phones, that photo hasn't actually gone anywhere yet. It has simply moved, and for a while at least, it's waiting somewhere you can still reach it.

Knowing exactly where deleted photos go, and for how long, turns a moment of panic into a calm few minutes of looking in the right places. This guide explains what "delete" really does on Android, where deleted photos end up in the most common apps, how long they stay there, and the point at which a deleted photo is gone for good.

## What "delete" actually does

On an Android phone, a photo is a file stored in the phone's internal storage (or on a memory card, if your phone has one). Gallery apps, photo apps and file managers all show you those files in different ways, and each one handles deletion in its own way.

These days, most of those apps don't erase a photo the moment you delete it. Instead, they move it to a **trash**, **bin**, **recycle bin** or **recently deleted** folder. The photo disappears from your normal view, but the file still exists. After a set period, the app removes it permanently.

That design exists because accidental deletion is incredibly common. A slip of the thumb while scrolling, a selection that included one photo too many, a child playing with the phone. The trash gives you a chance to undo it.

There's an important consequence, though: **deleted photos in the trash still take up storage space**. If you're deleting photos to free up room, you won't see the space come back until the trash is emptied. Our guide to [freeing up storage without losing anything](/daily-info/free-up-phone-storage) explains how to do that safely.

## What to do the moment you notice a photo is missing

If you've just realised a photo has gone, the order you look in matters. Work through these steps before trying anything else:

1. **Don't delete anything else.** Hold off on clearing space, emptying any trash folders or running cleaning apps until you've found what you're looking for.
2. **Search your gallery first.** Photos sometimes aren't deleted at all, just moved into another album, archived or hidden. Try searching by date, place or what's in the picture.
3. **Check your main gallery app's trash.** For most people, this is where the photo will be.
4. **Check Google Photos' trash too**, even if it isn't your main gallery app. It may be looking at the same photos.
5. **Check your file manager's trash**, if you ever delete files there.
6. **Check your cloud backup and its trash.**
7. **Check chats, email and other devices**, and ask anyone you shared the photo with.

The sections below explain each of these places in more detail.

## Google Photos

Google Photos is installed on most Android phones and is many people's main photo app. Its trash is found in the app's **Collections** or **Library** section, under **Trash** or **Bin**.

How long photos stay depends on whether they were backed up:

- **Backed-up photos and videos** stay in the trash for **60 days**.
- **Photos and videos that weren't backed up** stay for **30 days**.

Google Photos also treats deletion differently depending on backup. If a photo is backed up to your Google account and you delete it in the app, it's moved to the trash and removed everywhere: from your phone, from Google Photos online, and from any other devices signed in to the same account. That's sync at work. Our guide to [backup vs sync](/daily-info/backup-vs-sync) explains why that matters.

If you want to free up space on the phone but keep the backed-up copy, Google Photos has a separate option to remove only the copies stored on your device. Those photos stay safe in your Google account and can still be viewed in the app when you're online.

To restore a photo, open the trash, select it, and choose **Restore**. It returns to your library, and to your phone's gallery if it was stored on the phone.

## Samsung Gallery

Samsung phones come with their own Gallery app, which has a **Recycle bin**. You'll usually find it in the Gallery's menu (the three lines or three dots), or in the albums view.

Deleted photos and videos stay in the Recycle bin for **30 days** before being removed permanently. You can restore them individually or all at once.

The Samsung Gallery also lets you turn the Recycle bin off in its settings. If it's turned off, deleted photos are removed straight away, with no trash period at all. If you've ever changed this setting, or aren't sure, it's worth checking now, before you need it. Leaving it on costs a little storage, but it's the simplest protection against accidental deletion there is.

If you use Samsung's cloud features or have linked your Gallery to another cloud service, deleted items may also appear in that service's own trash. Check both if you're looking for something.

## Other phone makers' gallery apps

Most Android phone makers include their own gallery app, and most now include a recently deleted or trash folder. The names and locations vary, but they're usually found in one of these places:

- An album called **Recently deleted**, **Recycle bin** or **Trash** in the albums list.
- An option in the gallery's **menu** or **settings**.
- A section at the bottom of the albums screen.

Many of these keep deleted items for around **30 days**, though some use different periods and some let you change it. The quickest way to find out for your phone is to open the trash and look for a message explaining when items will be removed; many apps show the number of days left on each photo.

## Files by Google and other file managers

Photos aren't only deleted from gallery apps. If you manage files with a file manager, such as **Files by Google** or your phone maker's own **My Files** or **Files** app, deletions there are handled by that app.

Files by Google has a **Trash** that keeps deleted files for **30 days**. Other file managers often have a similar recycle bin, and some have none at all, deleting files immediately.

It's worth knowing that recent versions of Android include a system-wide trash feature that apps can use when deleting photos and other media. Items moved there are generally kept for around 30 days. This is one reason why a photo deleted in one app sometimes appears in the trash of another: several apps may be looking at the same underlying files.

## Hidden, archived and locked photos aren't deleted

Before assuming a photo has been deleted, check whether it's been tucked away instead. Several features remove photos from your main view without deleting them, and they're easy to trigger by accident.

- **Archive in Google Photos** moves photos out of your main photo view but keeps them in your library. You'll find them in the Archive section of the app's library.
- **Locked Folder in Google Photos** keeps sensitive photos behind your screen lock. Photos in the Locked Folder don't appear in your main view or in search, and aren't included in your backup by default, so they're worth knowing about for another reason too: if you remove the app's data or reset the phone without moving them out first, they can be lost.
- **Hidden albums and secure folders.** Many phone makers offer a hidden album or a secure, separate space for private photos, such as Samsung's Secure Folder. Photos moved there disappear from the normal gallery.

If a photo seems to have vanished without you deleting it, one of these is the most likely explanation.

## Deleting on the phone versus in the cloud

It helps to be clear about the two different things "delete" can mean when your photos are also stored online.

**Deleting from the phone and the cloud** is what usually happens when you delete a backed-up photo in a synced photo app. The photo goes to the trash and, after the trash period, is removed everywhere.

**Deleting from the phone only** is what you want when you're freeing up space but want to keep the photo. Some apps offer this as a separate option, often labelled something like "delete from device" or "free up space". The online copy stays safe.

**Deleting from the cloud only** can happen when you tidy up your library in a web browser or on another device. Depending on the service, that can also remove the photo from your phone the next time it syncs.

If you're not sure which one an app is about to do, read the confirmation message carefully before you tap. It usually tells you whether the photo will be removed from all your devices.

## Videos, screenshots and voice notes

Everything above applies to videos as well as photos. Videos are often the first thing people delete when their phone is full, because they're large, and they're also among the most painful to lose. They follow the same trash rules as photos in most apps, so check the trash for them in the same way.

Screenshots are usually stored in their own **Screenshots** folder and appear in your gallery like any other photo. Deleted screenshots go to the same trash.

Voice notes and audio recordings are different: they're often managed by the recording app or the chat app that created them, and may not appear in your gallery or its trash at all. Look in the app that made them.

## Chat apps and downloads

A photo you delete from your gallery may not be the only copy on your phone. Chat apps are a common hiding place.

**Photos you received in a chat** are usually stored inside that chat app, and often in a separate folder on the phone as well. Deleting a picture from your gallery doesn't delete it from the chat, and vice versa. Search the conversation, or look in the chat app's media or storage section.

**Photos you sent in a chat** are often still in the conversation, and on the phones of the people you sent them to. If a photo was shared, asking the recipient to send it back is often the fastest way to recover it. Just remember that photos sent through some chat apps are compressed, so the copy that comes back may be lower quality than the original.

**Downloaded images**, such as pictures saved from websites or emails, usually live in the **Downloads** folder, which some gallery apps don't show by default. Check your file manager.

## Cloud storage and other devices

If you back up or sync your photos to a cloud service, that service may still have a copy, or have it in its own trash.

Most cloud storage services keep deleted files for a limited time. OneDrive and Dropbox, for example, keep deleted files for a period that depends on the type of account, often around 30 days for personal plans. Google Drive keeps files in its trash for 30 days.

Also think about other devices: a tablet or computer signed in to the same account, an old phone in a drawer, or a computer you once copied photos to. Photos that have gone from one device can easily still be on another.

## Memory cards

Some Android phones still have a memory card slot. Photos saved to a memory card behave a little differently.

Whether a deleted photo on a memory card goes to a trash folder depends on the app you used to delete it. Photos deleted through an app with a trash usually go there, just like photos on internal storage. Photos deleted in other ways may be removed immediately.

If you're trying to recover photos from a memory card, stop using it straight away. Don't take new photos, and don't format the card, even if the phone suggests it. Formatting erases the card and makes recovery much harder.

## Why some photos skip the trash

Occasionally, a photo you're sure you deleted a few days ago isn't in any trash folder. There are a few common reasons:

- **The app you used has no trash.** Some file managers, older gallery apps and third-party apps delete files immediately.
- **The trash was turned off**, in an app that allows it.
- **The trash was emptied early**, either by you, by a cleaning app, or automatically because the phone was low on storage.
- **The photo was deleted from a computer**, while the phone was connected by cable. Files deleted this way usually bypass the phone's trash entirely.
- **The photo was on a memory card that was formatted or replaced.**

In these cases, the phone itself is unlikely to still have the photo, and a backup or another copy is the place to look.

## How long do deleted photos last?

Here's a summary of the most common places, and how long deleted items typically stay:

| Where it was deleted | Where to look | How long it's kept |
|---|---|---|
| Google Photos | Library or Collections, then Trash or Bin | 60 days if backed up, 30 days if not |
| Samsung Gallery | Recycle bin | 30 days (can be turned off) |
| Other gallery apps | Recently deleted, Trash or Recycle bin | Often around 30 days |
| Files by Google | Trash | 30 days |
| Cloud drives | The service's own trash or recycle bin | Varies by service and plan, often around 30 days |
{: .guide-table}

These periods can change with app updates, and some apps empty the trash sooner if your phone is running low on storage. If you're looking for a photo, don't wait: check as soon as you notice it's missing.

## When a deleted photo is really gone

A photo is truly gone when it has left every trash folder, and no other copy exists: not in a backup, not in the cloud, not in a chat and not on another device.

Once a photo has been permanently deleted from the phone's storage, getting it back is very difficult. The space it used is marked as free and gets reused for new data. Modern phone storage is also designed to clean up deleted space in the background, and modern Android phones encrypt their storage, which protects your privacy but means permanently deleted files can't realistically be pieced back together.

A **factory reset** erases everything on the phone in the same way. Photos that existed before a reset can only come back from a backup or another copy.

This is why recovery tools focus on the trash. ## Check your settings now

The best time to understand your phone's trash is before you need it. Take five minutes today:

- **Open your gallery app's trash** and see how long it keeps items.
- **Check that the trash is turned on**, if your gallery app lets you turn it off.
- **Check your file manager's trash**, if it has one.
- **Look at your cloud backup** and confirm it's running and up to date.
- **Make a mental note** of where each trash lives, so you know where to look if you ever delete something by mistake.

## Make deleting photos less scary

The trash is a safety net, not a plan. It only lasts a few weeks, and it empties itself. The only way to be sure a deleted photo can always be recovered is to have a copy that isn't affected when you delete it from your phone.

Keep your photos backed up, check the backup occasionally, and deleting photos to free up space, or by accident, stops being something to worry about.

## When you need to move files between phones

There are two very common moments when files need to get from one phone to another. The first is when you get a new phone and want your photos, videos and files to come with you. The second is when you want to share something with someone nearby: the photos from a family event, a video from a holiday, or a document a friend needs.

Both sound simple, and both can be surprisingly frustrating. Photos get compressed into small, blurry copies when they're sent through a chat app. Videos are too large to email. Cables don't fit, cloud uploads take hours, and it's not always clear whether everything actually arrived.

Smartphone All Data Transfer is built for these moments: it moves your photos, videos and files from one phone to another on Android. This guide covers the different ways files can travel between phones, what to check before you start, and how to make sure nothing gets left behind.

## The main ways files move between phones

There's more than one way to get files from one phone to another. Each has its strengths, and the right one depends on how much you're moving and where the other phone is.

| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Phone-to-phone transfer | Moving photos, videos and files directly from one phone to another | Keep both phones charged and nearby until it finishes |
| Cloud storage | Keeping a copy online and downloading it on the new phone | Large libraries take time to upload and download |
| Cable | Direct copying when you have the right cable | Needs a compatible cable or adapter |
| Chat or email | Sending one or two small files quickly | Photos and videos are often compressed, and size limits apply |
| Memory card | Phones that both have a card slot | Many phones no longer have one |
{: .guide-table}

### Sending through chat apps and email

Chat apps and email are convenient for a single photo, but they're not designed for moving a whole library. Many chat apps shrink photos and videos to save data, so what arrives is a lower-quality copy. Email usually has a limit on attachment size, which rules out most videos.

### Cloud storage

Uploading to cloud storage and downloading on the other phone works well, and has the bonus of leaving a backup behind. The downside is time: everything has to go up to the internet and then come back down, which for a large library can take hours and use a lot of data.

### Direct transfer

Moving files directly from one phone to the other skips the round trip through the internet that cloud storage needs, and avoids the size limits of email. That can make it the most practical option for large videos and big batches of photos, especially when both phones are in the same room.

## Before you start a transfer

A few minutes of preparation makes any transfer go more smoothly, whichever method you use.

- **Check the space on the receiving phone.** Look in its storage settings. If you're moving 40 GB of photos, the new phone needs more than 40 GB free.
- **Charge both phones.** A large transfer can take a while. Starting with full batteries, or keeping both phones plugged in, avoids a transfer stopping halfway.
- **Keep the phones close together** and stay put until the transfer finishes.
- **Tidy up first.** Deleting blurry shots, duplicates and videos you don't want means less to move and less to sort through later.
- **Close apps you don't need** on both phones, so the transfer isn't competing with other things.

## What moves easily, and what doesn't

Not everything on a phone is a file that can be copied across. It helps to know which things will move by transfer, and which need a different approach.

**Moves well by transfer:**

- Photos and screenshots
- Videos, including large ones
- Documents such as PDFs and Office files
- Other files stored on the phone, such as downloads and audio recordings

**Usually needs a different approach:**

- **Apps themselves.** These are reinstalled from the app store on the new phone rather than copied.
- **App data and settings.** Many apps keep data in an account that you sign in to on the new phone; some need their own backup or export.
- **Chat history.** Apps like WhatsApp use their own backup and transfer tools for your conversations.
- **Two-factor codes.** Authenticator apps need their own transfer, or each account set up again.
- **Contacts and calendars.** These usually sync through your account rather than being moved as files.

## Photos and videos: sizes and formats

Photos and videos are usually the bulk of any transfer, and they come with a couple of things worth knowing.

### Videos are big

A single minute of high-resolution video can take up hundreds of megabytes, and 4K video even more. That's why a phone with a few hundred videos can have a larger library than one with thousands of photos. When planning a transfer, videos are usually what decides how long it will take and how much space you'll need.

### Photo formats

Most Android phones save photos as JPEG files, which open almost everywhere. iPhones save photos in a format called HEIC by default, which keeps quality high while using less space. Most recent phones and computers can open HEIC files, but some older apps and devices can't. If a photo from an iPhone won't open, the format is the most likely reason. ## Planning a big move: a simple timeline

If you're moving everything to a new phone, spreading the work over a few days makes it calmer and much less likely that anything gets missed.

**A few days before the new phone arrives**

- Delete photos, videos and files you don't want to keep, so there's less to move.
- Back up your photos, videos, contacts and app data, and let the backup finish.
- Make a note of the apps you use and any that hold data you care about.

**The day you set up the new phone**

- Charge both phones and keep them nearby.
- Set up the new phone, sign in to your accounts, and install the apps you need.
- Transfer your photos, videos and files, then give the new phone time to finish processing them.

**The week after**

- Use the new phone normally, and keep the old one untouched.
- Whenever you notice something missing, move it across from the old phone.
- Once you're sure everything is there, sign out of your accounts on the old phone and reset it.

## Common transfer problems and how to avoid them

Most transfer problems have simple causes. Knowing them in advance saves a lot of frustration.

- **The transfer stops partway.** This is usually a flat battery, the phones moving apart or the screen locking during a long transfer. Keep both phones charged and close together, and check on them occasionally.
- **The new phone runs out of space.** Check the free space before you start. If the new phone has less storage than the old one, choose what to move rather than moving everything.
- **Photos appear in the wrong order.** Galleries usually sort by the date a photo was taken, which is stored inside the photo. If some appear out of place, give the gallery app time to finish scanning the new files.
- **Some files won't open.** The file may be in a format the new phone's apps don't support, like HEIC photos on some older apps. Try a different app to open it.
- **Duplicates appear.** Transferring the same photos twice, or transferring and restoring a backup, can leave two copies. Pick one method for each type of file.

## Sharing with someone nearby

Moving files to someone else's phone is a little different from moving them to your own. A few things are worth keeping in mind.

- **Only send what you mean to.** Double-check your selection before sending, especially when choosing many photos at once.
- **Only accept transfers you're expecting.** If a file arrives that you didn't ask for, don't open it.
- **Take care in public places.** On a busy train or in a café, make sure you're connecting to the phone you think you are.
- **Think about what's in the photos.** Pictures can include things like addresses, number plates or documents in the background.

## How long will a transfer take?

It's one of the first questions people ask, and the honest answer is: it depends almost entirely on how much you're moving. A few hundred photos can move in minutes. A library of tens of thousands of photos and hours of video can take much longer, whichever method you use.

A few things make the biggest difference:

- **The total size**, not the number of files. A handful of long videos can be larger than thousands of photos.
- **The method you choose.** Moving files directly between phones avoids waiting for an upload and a download, while cloud storage depends on your internet speed in both directions.
- **What else the phones are doing.** A phone that's also updating apps or syncing other data has less capacity left for the transfer.

To get an idea before you start, look at your phone's storage settings, which usually show how much space photos, videos and files take up. Moving in batches, such as one year of photos at a time, makes a long transfer easier to keep track of and easier to check afterwards.

## Moving files for someone else

Helping a parent, grandparent or friend move to a new phone is one of the most common reasons people look up how to transfer files. A few extra steps make it go smoothly.

- **Ask what matters most to them** before you start. For many people, it's photos of family and a few important contacts.
- **Make a backup first**, so nothing depends on the transfer going perfectly.
- **Write down their account details** somewhere safe, with their permission, since the new phone will ask for them.
- **Show them where things are** on the new phone once you've finished, especially their photos and messages.
- **Leave the old phone as it is** for a week or two, in case something turns up missing.

## Organising files on the new phone

A new phone is a rare chance to start tidy. Rather than recreating years of clutter, take a few minutes to set things up the way you'd like them.

- **Decide where downloads should go** and keep that folder tidy, deleting files once you've dealt with them.
- **Create a few folders for documents**, such as Home, Work and Travel, rather than leaving everything in one place.
- **Review your albums.** Albums from the old phone may come across as folders. Keep the ones you use, and merge or remove the rest.
- **Clear out chat media.** Photos and videos saved automatically by chat apps can take up a lot of space. Keep the ones that matter and let the rest go.

## After the transfer: check before you delete

The most important step comes after the transfer finishes. Before you delete anything from the old phone, or hand it on, check that what you moved really arrived.

- **Compare the counts.** Look at how many photos and videos are on each phone. They should roughly match for what you moved.
- **Open a sample.** Scroll back through different months and years, and open a few photos and videos to make sure they play and look right.
- **Check your important files.** Open any documents and recordings you particularly care about.
- **Look for folders you might have missed**, such as screenshots, downloads and pictures saved from chat apps.

Only when you're happy that everything is there should you delete files from the old phone or reset it. Keeping the old phone untouched for a week or two after switching is a good safety net: if you notice something missing, it's still there.

## Moving files and keeping a backup

A transfer and a backup do different jobs. A transfer moves files from one phone to another, so they're on the new device. A backup keeps a separate copy somewhere safe, so that if a phone is lost or broken, your files aren't lost with it.

When you switch phones, it's worth doing both. Transfer your files so they're on your new phone, and make sure they're also backed up, so you're not relying on a single device.
